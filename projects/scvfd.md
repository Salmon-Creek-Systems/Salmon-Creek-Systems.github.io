# Project Overview

## Describe how the project provides direct benefits to Tribes, Economically Disadvantaged Communities, or Severely Disadvantaged Communities and list these communities. [NCRP Interactive Map]
The Salmon Creek community in remote Southern Humboldt County is largely just outside the mapped Severely Disadvantaged Community designation shared by neighbor communities including Miranda, Myers Flat, Briceland and the Mattole. Cooperation with these neighbors means they benefit from improving SCVFD's operational readiness and ability to coordinate. Recent economic change has made it hard to fund the fire prevention and planning and what resources exist have gone first to physical gear and response capability - important, but leaving mapping, data, and interoperability with neighboring agencies underfunded. This project addresses that gap.


## Describe the public benefits this project provides.
The project uses high-quality public and agency data to produce maps and map data — but in these remote areas that data is often incomplete or inaccurate: roads are wrong or missing, and there is no standard way to indicate water sources useful for firefighting. Correcting this data and making it available to local firefighters is an obvious benefit, but so is making it available to neighboring and regional organizations.

As a concrete example, a neighboring mutual-aid department assisting SCVFD recently elected to drive back to their own water sources because they lacked information on local ones — causing at least a forty-minute delay for the water tender. There are roughly 150 ponds in the SCVFD service area. Providing rapid, clear information about the local landscape greatly improves interagency cooperation during incidents. CAL FIRE has already asked the local VFD to help improve their maps, especially for remote roads.

The project is built for sharing across organizational and technical boundaries. The same maintained data can be published into the operational tools partners already use — for example, SCVFD tracks unit locations and response state in the “IamResponding” app, and this project lets the department push more accurate local road and water-source data into that platform. The atlas's federation capability lets neighboring departments, stewardship groups, and agencies pull the specific layers they need into their own systems, so the effort of collecting local knowledge once benefits the whole community.



## Describe the specific technical assistance needed to fully develop the project and why your organization needs technical assistance. What financial and/or capacity needs in your organization does this TA address?
This project requires GIS and geospatial-data expertise that an entirely volunteer organization does not have in-house. The technical assistance is a discrete, well-defined body of data and mapping work performed by the TA provider:

Compiling and quality-checking geospatial data from public, agency, and local-knowledge sources — roads, addressing, water sources and draftability, fire safety zones, and access — at parcel, district, and regional scales;
Configuring the Fire Atlas for the SCVFD district and producing the deliverable outputs: a printed gazetteer, standard-format map files, and an interactive web atlas (detailed in the next section);
Setting up the non-specialist editing and update tools — web map, shared spreadsheets, mobile photo submission — so SCVFD and community members can keep the data current themselves; and
Enabling controlled data sharing and federation with partner agencies and tools.

Hiring a consultant to produce a one-time set of maps would not meet the need: without a way to update the data as third-party and local information changes, the maps quickly go obsolete. The gap this TA addresses is specific and technical — the specialized GIS and cloud-platform skills required to build the atlas and stand up the tools — not the ongoing operation of the department, which the volunteers carry themselves once the tools are in hand. The requested TA amount is $15,000.


# Describe what you expect to achieve or leverage with the current technical assistance opportunity. 
The primary output is the Salmon Creek Fire Atlas: a collection of spatial features supporting SCVFD's use cases, with data that is accurate, comprehensive, and actionable for incident-management needs. It includes roads and access; water sources and draftability; landmarks, LPSS sections, and accurate street addresses for communicating with other agencies; and private information such as gate combinations.

The Atlas is delivered in a range of synchronized formats, all generated from the same underlying data and straightforward to update and refresh:

A printed gazetteer optimized for efficient visual use in the field — so a team can use it and then hand it to an assisting crew, knowing they have a self-contained guide to local water and road data;
Standard-format map files (e.g., GeoPackage) that other organizations and agencies can pull directly into their own GIS systems; and
An interactive web platform for curating, improving, and updating the data, and for making it useful to other community organizations.

The Fire Atlas is already proving useful as a way to gather and operationalize community knowledge and integrate it with public and agency data. Non-specialists can not only use it but improve it — through shared spreadsheets, web-based editors, and mobile forms — with the community controlling whether potentially sensitive data is shared outside the department. The underlying data is stored as simple, version-controlled features with linked tables, so SCVFD can hold and maintain these assets into the future as a repository that can be forked, copied, and merged with any tool that reads the underlying files.

The project is also designed to be leveraged into downstream funding and planning. We are already working with a local Stewardship Council and Road Committee on grants to improve area roads that serve both the VFD and the community, and we expect to support applications for fuels reduction, fire safety and response, and land conservation — including oak-ecosystem restoration. Many of these efforts begin at the parcel or individual-landowner scale, but funding agencies clearly prefer them aggregated into larger, coordinated proposals. The Atlas's multi-scale data model, federation features, and ability to generate whatever outputs an application needs — text, maps, spreadsheets — are built to solve exactly that aggregation problem, letting the region assemble parcel-scale local knowledge into competitive regional applications.

The result is a Fire Atlas that SCVFD and the community own and can maintain and distribute themselves — able to pull in outside data, with a simple web interface to manage versions, edits, and sharing — accurate and useful while respecting community privacy.

# Goal Mapping

## Capacity: Data, Analysis, Monitoring
Combining local knowledge with public and agency datasets produces maps and planning data that are more accurate, current, and actionable. This project delivers immediately useful, quality-checked maps and datasets — road geometries, addressing, water sources and their draftability, and fire safety zones — together with the platform and tools for Salmon Creek's VFD and community to correct and extend them themselves. The data is structured so it can be analyzed at whatever scale a decision requires, and so the community can keep it refreshed as source data and local conditions change.

## Capacity: Regional and Local Planning
This project supports local prefire planning and incident response within the Salmon Creek district by giving SCVFD and its partners improved geospatial assets and the means to maintain and share them. Because the Atlas aggregates cleanly from the individual parcel up to district and regional extents, it also feeds partners' larger, multi-landowner planning efforts and grant applications, where funders increasingly ask that parcel-scale work be assembled into a coordinated regional picture. The range of output formats directly assists grantwriting, document creation, and data interoperation.

## Capacity: Organizational Support and Funding
The technical assistance provides specialized GIS and cloud platform expertise as a "living" atlas: it does not deliver a one-time set of maps but maintainable data infrastructure and training that let SCVFD and its partners keep the data current and put it to use themselves. Atlas data is always open and available rather than being locked to a specific vendor - reducing future cost and risk.

## Capacity: Community Engagement
A central goal of this project is to establish local, sustainable data stewardship. The project gives non-specialists commonly available tools — web-map editing, shared spreadsheets, and mobile photo submission — to add, correct, and improve the data, enabling community knowledge coproduction by people without GIS training. Local knowledge stays in local hands, with the community in control of whether potentially sensitive information (such as gate combinations) is shared beyond the department.
Community Health and Safety: Fire Preparedness
This project enhances SCVFD's ability to respond and to have vital information available during fire incidents. It supports planning of access and evacuation routes, cataloging of draftable water sources, and identification of structures and their wildfire exposure. Accurate, shareable local data measurably shortens response time and improves coordination with mutual-aid and agency partners during active incidents. Atlas data can be pushed directly into the tools already used for real-time response, such as IamResponding.
